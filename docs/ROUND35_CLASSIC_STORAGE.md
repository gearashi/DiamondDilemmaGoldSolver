# Classic checkpoint storage

The installed legacy checkpoint has 131,072 lanes and 43 NumPy members. The benchmark opened it read-only, rewrote isolated copies, and verified the uncompressed SHA-256 of every member. A stored ZIP wrote 260,191,435 bytes, level-one DEFLATE wrote 42,362,829, and level six wrote 39,157,703. Measured times were 1.40, 3.35 and 13.52 seconds. These are process I/O counters on Windows. They include file-format overhead and do not measure physical SSD wear.

The production writer now uses level-one DEFLATE for signed classic checkpoints. The file remains a standalone NPZ with its original names, dtypes, shapes and Fortran-order metadata. Hashing covers the final ZIP bytes in write order. The writer flushes the temporary file, fsyncs it, writes the generation receipt, then replaces the public checkpoint. Immutable frontier files retain their original layout. Fault injection at the member write, fsync, receipt write and final rename preserved the prior generation and its receipt. The v1.14 public solver verification preserved 480 jobs and 230,400 candidates on terminal reopen, rejected a changed RNG and rejected a shortened frontier. The archive and runner regression suite passed on Python 3.12 and 3.15. The released CUDA checkout ran under Python 3.12.

Five genuine CUDA generations with 128 active lanes wrote 106,196 bytes as level-one archives. At 2,048 lanes with 128 occupied, the total was 237,441 bytes, and with all lanes occupied it was 845,631. An independently verified content-addressed SQLite prototype at four chunk sizes wrote more bytes on every active-pool control. On the installed checkpoint the three chunk sizes wrote between 87,865,872 and 174,905,776 bytes. [Installed snapshot](R35_CLASSIC_INSTALLED_STORAGE.json) · [Five active generations](R35_CLASSIC_WRITES.json)

Parking 2,035 jobs while running one lane changed the outcome. The 64-KiB SQLite variant wrote 698,520 bytes across five saves versus 1,048,865 for the compressed standalone archive. With 8,001 parked jobs and 128 lanes, the 256-KiB variant wrote 2,152,752 versus 3,759,525. A more specialized experiment stored the verified frozen bank once and recorded its suffix offset in the active archive. Including a full portable final save, this wrote 446,739 versus 1,056,665 bytes on the first matched comparison and 1,582,430 versus 3,767,325 on the second. Without parked work both paths wrote 113,996 bytes. Every reconstructed field was compared with its source. Both genuine sequences retained the whole bank over the five saves. The frozen-bank extension remains open for actual suffix consumption and sharded ownership, publication interruption, authenticated bank corruption, portable stop at an already saved generation, old profiles, sidecar retirement and free-space failure. ## Immutable bank interval writer

Keep the first full authenticated checkpoint as a portable base. The next qualifying interval links that exact archive under its SHA-256 in the same profile, then saves only active fields and the exact remaining bank suffix. The first link is verified from disk. A reused link whose file identity changes gets checked again. A changed bank, an unsupported filesystem, or an unsuitable compressed fraction uses the full format. The active head and a full stop use different receipt names so a failed same-generation repack preserves the prior valid head. A normal stop publishes a standalone NPZ and retires obsolete private links. A same-generation final repack uses the authenticated on-disk interval and requires zero new GPU lane reads.

An actual CUDA process was interrupted after publishing a 1,911-job external bank at generation 298. A new 64-lane process restored it with verified origin and stopped at generation 1,456 in a standalone archive. It retained all 2,051 job owners across the ledger's completed, active, paused and unassigned counts. A second original workload consumed 512 of 2,176 initially parked jobs across four intervals. CUDA, Vulkan and Direct3D 12 restored the active and frozen stacks, resized them to 16 lanes, advanced them and matched the control on all 38 native fields. The five-save comparison, including a full stop, wrote 140,481 versus 240,254 bytes on this changing bank. A stable 8,001-job bank wrote 1,616,309 versus 3,766,695. [Process crash](R35_PARKED_CRASH.json) · [Reassignment and cross-backend restart](R35_PARKED_PUBLIC_FINAL.json)

Writing every available bank reference regressed when 480 active lanes shared only 32 parked jobs. The final policy requires at least 16 parked jobs and four KiB of compressed bank data. It admits a small fraction only after observing enough interval saves to cover one portable repack, using a bounded horizon of three to sixteen saves. At 33 generations the 512-to-480 case now ties standalone output at 3,450,760 bytes. The 2,048-to-1,500 case saves 1,178,915 bytes and the 4,096-to-2,560 case saves 4,074,576. One to four parked jobs stay on the portable path. These costs are from a stopped snapshot at an already saved generation, which exercises the extra full repack. Native snapshot validation now reads the captured states and prefix floors directly. This removes two extra device readbacks and validates the very arrays written. The separate field-transfer investigation remains active. The exact CUDA difference prototype used a full device mirror and transferred 26,010,552 of 54,231,040 bytes on the long fully occupied 32,768-lane control at four-KiB chunks. Range-based readers derive unchanged regions from native ownership and the greatest attained frame depth. Compare them on older stacks, reassignment, current-policy inputs, normal intervals and available backends before choosing the production readback path.
