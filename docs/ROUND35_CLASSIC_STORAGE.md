# Classic checkpoint storage

The installed legacy checkpoint has 131,072 lanes and 43 NumPy members. The benchmark opened it read-only, rewrote isolated copies, and verified the uncompressed SHA-256 of every member. A stored ZIP wrote 260,191,435 bytes, level-one DEFLATE wrote 42,362,829, and level six wrote 39,157,703. Measured times were 1.40, 3.35 and 13.52 seconds. These are process I/O counters on Windows. They include file-format overhead and do not measure physical SSD wear.

The production writer now uses level-one DEFLATE for signed classic checkpoints. The file remains a standalone NPZ with its original names, dtypes, shapes and Fortran-order metadata. Hashing covers the final ZIP bytes in write order. The writer flushes the temporary file, fsyncs it, writes the generation receipt, then replaces the public checkpoint. Immutable frontier files retain their original layout. Fault injection at the member write, fsync, receipt write and final rename preserved the prior generation and its receipt. The v1.14 public solver verification preserved 480 jobs and 230,400 candidates on terminal reopen, rejected a changed RNG and rejected a shortened frontier. The archive and runner regression suite passed on Python 3.12 and 3.15. The released CUDA checkout ran under Python 3.12.

Five genuine CUDA generations with 128 active lanes wrote 106,196 bytes as level-one archives. At 2,048 lanes with 128 occupied, the total was 237,441 bytes, and with all lanes occupied it was 845,631. An independently verified content-addressed SQLite prototype at four chunk sizes wrote more bytes on every active-pool control. On the installed checkpoint the three chunk sizes wrote between 87,865,872 and 174,905,776 bytes. [Installed snapshot](R35_CLASSIC_INSTALLED_STORAGE.json) · [Five active generations](R35_CLASSIC_WRITES.json)

Parking 2,035 jobs while running one lane changed the outcome. The 64-KiB SQLite variant wrote 698,520 bytes across five saves versus 1,048,865 for the compressed standalone archive. With 8,001 parked jobs and 128 lanes, the 256-KiB variant wrote 2,152,752 versus 3,759,525. A more specialized experiment stored the verified frozen bank once and recorded its suffix offset in the active archive. Including a full portable final save, this wrote 446,739 versus 1,056,665 bytes on the first matched comparison and 1,582,430 versus 3,767,325 on the second. Without parked work both paths wrote 113,996 bytes. Every reconstructed field was compared with its source. Both genuine sequences retained the whole bank over the five saves. The frozen-bank extension remains open for actual suffix consumption and sharded ownership, publication interruption, authenticated bank corruption, portable stop at an already saved generation, old profiles, sidecar retirement and free-space failure.
